THE BIGGER PICTURE

Football must communicate beyond colour.

Cones, bibs, balls, markers and kits are information. Making them distinguishable with contrast, shapes and labels can help players and coaches react confidently. This original educational experience was created by Cornelius Aurelius as part of his Colour Blindness in Football awareness work.

These scenarios illustrate accessibility principles, not a diagnosis or a guarantee that any particular colour combination is safe for everyone. For practical guidance, see The FA’s colour-blindness guidance and The FA/UEFA football guidance booklet.

COACH’S FIELD GUIDE

HOW TO PLAY

When cone colours are similar, a target is hidden against grass or bibs look alike, choose Colour risk. If strong contrast is supported by clear numbers, symbols or patterns, choose Clear setup.

On every choice you receive an explanation. Matchday offers three chances and scores points; Practice lets you learn without penalties.

Use buttons on touchscreens or 1 / 2 on a keyboard. Use Enter to continue. Press Esc to pause or resume.

Grayscale is a simple luminance check, not an accurate simulation of colour vision deficiency.
